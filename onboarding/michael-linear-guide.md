## How to Assign Work to Ella

The assignment is the most important move you make. A well-assigned batch = smooth week. A sloppy assignment = confusion, rework, and Ella fatigue.

### Batch Size: 5 Tasks. Never 6. Never 4. Five.

The number isn't arbitrary. Five tasks:

- Fits in ~1 hour of Ella's focused time
- Prevents the psychological paralysis of a long "Assigned to Me" list
- Lets the AI process in parallel (voice memo on task 1 gets transcribed while she's on task 2)
- Creates a natural cadence: assign → complete → celebrate → assign next

**Exception:** If Ella explicitly requests a smaller batch (fatigue, busy week), drop to 3. Never go above 5 unless she asks for more.

### Clear Scope Is Everything

Every batch must have a **theme** and a **boundary**. Ella thrives when she knows exactly what "done" looks like.

**✅ Good Batch Assignments:**

> "Batch 3: North Shore Voice Memos. 5 interview tasks — all recording, no reviews. ~10 min each. You covered the Windward side last week so these build on that momentum. Order doesn't matter."

> "Batch 4: Draft Review. 5 AI-written articles ready for your eyes. Focus on: parking accuracy, hours of operation, and local vibe check. No voice memos this batch."

> "Batch 5: Mixed. 3 voice memos (GRO-150, 151, 152) + 2 reviews (GRO-148, 149). Do the voice memos first so the AI has time to process while you review."

**❌ Bad Batch Assignments:**

> "Here's some stuff — do whatever order you want."

Too vague. No theme, no boundary, no expectations set.

> "15 new tasks for you! Take your time."

Violates the 5-task rule. Even if you say "take your time," seeing 15 tasks creates pressure.

> "These are all urgent."

Everything can't be urgent. If everything is priority 1, nothing is. Sequence them.

### Sprint Planning: The Cycle Board Method

Every 2 weeks, plan the Production Cycle:

1. **Review last cycle:** What shipped? What stalled? What patterns emerged in Ella's feedback?
2. **Select 10-15 tasks** for the cycle (2-3 batches of 5). These go into the Production Cycle.
3. **Sequence the batches** using the confidence gradient (see below).
4. **Check capacity.** Is Ella traveling? Busy week? Adjust batch timing.
5. **Set the first batch live.** Assign 5 tasks to Ella. Leave the remaining 10 in Todo (not assigned).

**The Confidence Gradient for Sequencing:**

```
Batch 1: Topics she knows COLD (home beach, favorite spots)
    ↓
Batch 2: Adjacent topics (nearby areas, related activities)
    ↓
Batch 3: Synthesis/comparison topics (draws on Batch 1 + 2 knowledge)
    ↓
Batch 4: Reviews of AI drafts (she's now deeply familiar with the content)
```

Don't front-load comparison or synthesis tasks. Build her confidence with easy wins first.

**Task Assignment Checklist (Before Hitting Assign):**
- [ ] Batch size = exactly 5
- [ ] Each task has a clear, single deliverable
- [ ] Task descriptions are ≤ 5 sentences (any longer → split or clarify)
- [ ] Review tasks have a specific checklist (3-5 bullet points of what to look for)
- [ ] Voice memo tasks have the questions attached or linked
- [ ] Batch has a theme (communicated in the first task description or a batch note)

## The Weekly Sync: 10 Minutes That Prevent Everything

This is your most important recurring meeting. It's short, it's casual, and if you skip it, problems compound silently.

### The 3-Part Agenda (10 minutes total)

**Part 1: Celebrate Wins (3 minutes)**

Tell her specifically what she caught that was valuable. Be concrete:

> "That catch on the Kailua parking situation — you saved us from publishing bad info during peak visitor season. That's exactly why you're in the loop."

> "Your story about the turtle encounter in the Mokulua piece — that detail isn't on any other website. The AI can't generate that. That's your fingerprint on the content."

Why this matters: Ella has what we call an **open G Center** — she doesn't have a fixed sense of identity or self-worth. She needs external reflection to know what she's good at. Telling her what she did well isn't just nice — it's fueling her ability to keep doing good work. Without this, she'll drift toward uncertainty and second-guessing.

**Part 2: Preview the Next Batch (4 minutes)**

> "Next up: 5 voice memos covering the North Shore spots — Waimea Bay, Shark's Cove, Pipeline, Sunset Beach, and the food trucks in Haleiwa. You've surfed up there, so these should be in your wheelhouse. I'm aiming to get those to you by Wednesday. No reviews this batch — just recording."

Give her:
- The topic area
- The task type (voice memos vs reviews)
- Approximate timing
- Any context about why this batch matters

This removes the surprise factor. Ella operates best when she can mentally prepare. Surprise assignments feel like demands; previewed assignments feel like teamwork.

**Part 3: Listen for Fatigue (3 minutes)**

Ask open-ended questions and LISTEN. Don't problem-solve immediately:

> "How's the pace feeling this week?"

> "Any tasks that felt particularly draining?"

> "Is the 5-task batch still feeling right or should we adjust?"

Watch for these verbal signals:
- "It's fine" (translation: it might not be fine, but I don't want to be difficult)
- "I've been pretty busy with [other thing]" (translation: Active Oahu might be slipping)
- "I'll try to get to it" (translation: I'm behind and feeling guilty)
- Long pauses before answering (translation: I'm processing whether I can be honest)

When you hear these: reduce batch size or switch to review-only tasks for the next round. Proactively offer: "Want me to cut the next batch to 3 so you have more breathing room?"

## Ella's Design-Aware Workflow

Ella operates differently from someone with a defined willpower or intuition center. Understanding her design is the difference between flowing work and constant friction.

### Open Ego (Open Heart Center): No Fixed Willpower

**What this means:** Ella doesn't have a consistent, reliable source of willpower. She can't "power through" on discipline alone. Her energy comes in waves — some days she's on, some days she's off. This isn't laziness; it's her design.

**How to work with it:**
- **Don't overload.** 5 tasks per batch is the ceiling because willpower is a finite daily resource for her. More than 5 and she'll start marking things "Done" without proper review just to clear the queue.
- **Don't make promises on her behalf.** She'll say yes to things in the moment because she doesn't have a fixed sense of what's "too much." You're the gatekeeper.
- **Batch variety helps.** Mixing voice memos and reviews keeps the energy fresh. 5 straight hours of either one is draining.
- **Never ask "when can you get to this?"** It triggers people-pleasing answers. Instead: "This batch is ready — no rush, the AI will process whenever you get to it."

### Open Spleen (Open Splenic Center): No Fixed Intuition

**What this means:** Ella doesn't have reliable spontaneous knowing. She can't glance at a draft and instantly know if something is wrong — she needs to go through a checklist. Her intuition is inconsistent and environmentally influenced.

**How to work with it:**
- **Give her checklists, not open-ended "does this feel right?"** questions. A checklist of 3-5 specific things to verify anchors her review process.
- **Don't ask "what's your gut say?"** — her gut doesn't have a consistent voice. Ask "does the parking section match what you know?" instead.
- **She may hold onto drafts longer than necessary** because she doesn't trust her own assessment. A gentle "I think you caught everything — want me to move this to Done?" can help.
- **Environment matters.** She'll make better calls after a good night's sleep, in a calm setting, not rushed. The 24-hour rule exists because of this.

### Open G Center: Needs Reflection

**What this means:** Ella doesn't have a fixed sense of identity or direction. She doesn't automatically know "I'm good at X" or "my role is Y." She needs external mirrors to reflect back who she is and what she contributes.

**How to work with it:**
- **Tell her what she did well — specifically.** Every sync. "You caught the street direction error in the Lanikai piece" is 10x more valuable than "great work this week."
- **Connect her contributions to outcomes.** "Because you flagged the sunscreen law, that article now ranks for 'reef safe sunscreen Hawaii' — that's actual traffic."
- **Don't ask "what do you want to work on next?"** — she may genuinely not know. Offer options: "Next batch could be more North Shore voice memos or draft reviews — which sounds better?"
- **Praise is fuel, not flattery.** It's literally how she knows what direction she's heading. Without it, she drifts.

### The Combined Effect: A Workflow That Works

| Her Design | Don't Do This | Do This Instead |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Open Ego | Assign more than 5 tasks, push for speed, ask her to "power through" | Batch of 5, flexible timing, respect energy waves |
| Open Spleen | Ask for gut checks, rush decisions, expect instant knowing | Provide checklists, encourage the 24-hour rule, structure reviews |
| Open G | Give generic praise, ask "what do you want to do?", leave her without feedback | Specific praise every sync, offer options (not open questions), reflect her value |

## The Interview Pipeline: Task Flow Architecture

Understanding the exact movement of tasks through stages lets you diagnose bottlenecks instantly.

### Full Flow Diagram

```
STAGE 0: PLANNING (You)
You create task in Linear → set assignee to "AI System" → task in Backlog
        ↓
STAGE 1: QUESTION GENERATION (AI)
AI reads topic → generates 7-8 interview questions → posts in task description
→ reassigns to Ella → status: Todo → appears in her "Assigned to Me"
        ↓
STAGE 2: VOICE MEMO (Ella)
Ella reads questions → records 5-10 min voice memo → comments with audio
→ status: In Review → AI picks up
        ↓
STAGE 3: DRAFTING (AI)
AI transcribes audio → cross-references with research → writes full draft
→ posts draft in comments → status: In Progress → reassigns to Ella
        ↓
STAGE 4: REVIEW (Ella)
Ella reads draft → checks against checklist → comments with corrections
→ IF GOOD: status: Done → moves to publishing queue
→ IF CHANGES NEEDED: status: In Progress → AI picks up for revision
        ↓
STAGE 5: REVISION LOOP (AI ↔ Ella)
AI applies corrections → reposts → Ella reviews again → loop until Done
        ↓
STAGE 6: PUBLISH (AI)
AI formats final → SEO optimization → internal linking → marks complete
```

### Pipeline States and Your Intervention Points

| State | Owner | Normal Duration | Intervention Trigger | What To Do |
|-------|-------|----------------|---------------------|------------|
| Backlog | You | Indefinite | — | Assign when ready |
| Todo | Ella | 1-3 days | > 5 days untouched | Check in: "Everything okay with this batch?" |
| In Progress (voice memo) | Ella | 1-2 hours | > 3 days | She may be stuck on the questions — offer to simplify |
| In Review (AI drafting) | AI | 2-6 hours | > 24 hours | System may not have triggered — ping AI or reassign |
| In Progress (review) | Ella | 1-2 days | > 4 days | She may not trust her review — offer checklist |
| Blocked | Either | Variable | > 2 days | Direct intervention required — DM her |

### Common Pipeline Failures and Fixes

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| Task stuck in Todo for 5+ days | Ella overwhelmed or forgot about batch | Weekly check-in catch. Reduce next batch. |
| AI didn't pick up after Ella's comment | Comment too vague for AI trigger, or system delay | Check comment clarity. If good, reassign task to trigger AI. |
| Draft returned for revision 3+ times | Scope unclear or AI missing context | Review the original task description. Did it provide enough direction? Add context as a comment. |
| Ella marking everything Done quickly | Fatigue or disengagement | Reduce batch. Switch to voice memos only (less cognitive load than review). Check in. |
| Ella not commenting at all, just status changes | She doesn't understand comments drive the AI | Remind her: "The AI reads your comments to know what to do — even a one-sentence note helps." |

## How to Review Ella's Work

You're not reviewing her comments for grammar. You're checking the pipeline health and output quality. Review at the batch level, not the individual task level.

### What to Review

**Every batch (weekly):**
- [ ] Did she complete all 5 tasks within a reasonable window (3-5 days)?
- [ ] Are her comments specific enough for the AI to act on? (Scan 2-3 comments per batch)
- [ ] Did she catch things the AI missed? (If yes, celebrate in sync)
- [ ] Are there any tasks where the review cycle went 3+ rounds? (Indicates a scope or clarity problem)
- [ ] Is the output quality — the final AI-generated content — improving?

**Spot-check tasks (monthly):**
- [ ] Pick one reviewed article. Read it as a user. Does it feel authoritative?
- [ ] Compare the AI draft (before Ella review) vs. final. What did she actually catch?
- [ ] Are there any factual errors that slipped through? (If yes, update the review checklist)

### What NOT to Micromanage

- **Her word choice in comments.** "The parking situation is bad" is fine; it doesn't need to be "parking availability is severely constrained during peak hours." The AI handles polishing.
- **The order she does tasks within a batch.** As long as all 5 get done, sequencing is her call.
- **Her response time down to the hour.** As long as a batch turns around in 3-5 days, she's on track.
- **Whether she uses voice memos or text.** Some days she might prefer typing. It doesn't matter — the AI handles both.
- **How "professional" her comments sound.** Casual, conversational comments actually produce better AI output than stiff, formal ones.

### The Quality Signal to Watch

The single most important metric: **Is Ella catching things the AI can't?** If the answer is yes on 2+ tasks per batch, she's delivering exactly the value she's there for. If 0 per batch, something is off — either the tasks are too simple, the AI is over-performing (unlikely), or she's disengaged.

## Signs She's Overwhelmed (And Exact Interventions)

Catch these early. Late intervention = burnout = content pipeline stops.

| Signal | What It Looks Like | Intervention |
|--------|-------------------|--------------|
| **Comment Shrink** | "Looks good" on a draft with obvious issues. Comments drop from 3-4 sentences to 1-2 words. | Reduce batch to 3. Switch to voice memos only (less cognitive load). Say: "Noticing the reviews might be draining — want me to skip reviews this batch?" |
| **Response Time Drop** | Normal batch turnover: 3 days. Suddenly: 7+ days with no communication. | DM her directly. "Hey, no pressure — just checking if the current batch is feeling heavy. Happy to adjust." |
| **Clarification Silence** | She normally asks 1-2 clarifying questions per batch. Suddenly: zero questions, zero comments beyond bare minimum. | She's either confused or checked out. Call it out gently: "I noticed no questions on this batch — everything clear or should I add more detail?" |
| **In Progress Pile-Up** | 3+ tasks stuck in In Progress with no movement for 4+ days. | She's stuck and doesn't know how to unstick. Offer: "Want me to walk through one of these with you on a quick call?" |
| **Repeated "I'll get to it"** | Same batch, third time she's said she'll do it soon. | She's avoiding. Reduce scope drastically. "Let's make this batch just 2 tasks — pick whichever ones feel easiest. No rush." |
| **Approving Without Reading** | Multiple reviews marked Done within minutes of each other, with identical brief comments. | Burnout or overwhelm. Pause assignments entirely. "Taking a batch break this week — you've earned it. We'll pick up fresh next week." |
| **Emotional Tone Shift** | Comments go from warm/engaged to flat/curt. | She's depleted. Don't dig in — pull back. "The content can wait. Want to skip the sync this week too and just take the space?" |

### The Golden Rule of Intervention

**Always frame reductions as your decision, not her failure.** Never say "you seem overwhelmed." Say "I want to lighten the load this week" or "let's run a smaller batch while I catch up on the AI side." Protect her from feeling like she's letting anyone down.

## Communication Cadence

When to talk, when to shut up, and what channel to use.

### The Rhythm

| Frequency | Channel | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| **Weekly** | Voice or video call (10 min) | The sync: celebrate, preview, listen |
| **As-needed (low urgency)** | Linear task comments | Clarifications, status updates, praise on specific catches |
| **As-needed (medium urgency)** | DM (Telegram/Slack) | "Quick check — GRO-144 ready for review, whenever you get to it" |
| **As-needed (high urgency)** | DM with clear subject | "Need your call on Mokulua draft by Thursday — lmk if that timeline works" |
| **Never** | Between 8pm-8am her time | Unless she messages you first. Respect her off-hours. |
| **Never** | Email | She doesn't check it. Linear and DMs only. |

### When to Ping vs. Let Her Work

**Ping when:**
- A task has been in In Progress for 5+ days with no update
- You notice a quality signal (comment shrink, approval speed-up) and want to check in
- A task in the pipeline is blocking 3+ downstream tasks
- She hasn't acknowledged a batch assignment after 2 days
- You're previewing the next batch (part of weekly sync)

**Don't ping when:**
- A task has been In Progress for 2-3 days (normal cadence)
- You're curious about progress (wait for the weekly sync)
- You have a new idea for content (queue it; don't interrupt her current batch)
- You want to say "great job" on something (save for the sync — mid-week praise can feel like pressure)

### Praise Protocol

**Public praise (Linear comments):**
> "Ella caught a critical detail here — the parking situation changed in 2023 and this draft had the old info. Great catch. Using this version."

Why: The AI logs it, she sees it in context, and it reinforces the human-in-the-loop value. Also gives the AI a signal that the corrected version is the final one.

**Private praise (weekly sync):**
> "That Mokulua piece — your story about the turtle encounter. I checked and no other site has content like that. That's a ranking signal, and it came from you."

Why: Connects her contribution to business outcomes. Feeds the G Center.

**What not to do:**
- Generic praise ("great job this week") — doesn't tell her what she did well
- Praise that contradicts your own review standards ("looks perfect" when there were 3 corrections needed)
- Praise as a prelude to criticism ("great work, BUT we need to talk about your speed")

## The Cycle Board: Sprint Planning in Practice

### Foundation → Production → Growth → Empire

The four ventures map to four cycle boards. Here's how to use them for sprint planning:

```
Week 1 (Sprint Start)
├── Foundation: AI tooling, SEO audits, keyword research — (You + AI)
├── Production: Assign Batch 1 (5 tasks to Ella) — (Ella + AI)
├── Growth: Distribution planning — (You, no Ella)
└── Empire: Research, ideation — (You, no Ella)

Week 1.5 (Mid-Sprint Check)
├── Foundation: Audit progress
├── Production: Check — did Ella complete Batch 1? Any stuck tasks?
├── Growth: Content ready for distribution pipeline?
└── Empire: Any findings worth escalating?

Week 2 (Sprint Close)
├── Foundation: Deploy tooling improvements
├── Production: Assign Batch 2 (5 tasks). Review Batch 1 output quality.
├── Growth: Schedule social posts, outreach
└── Empire: File findings for next sprint planning
```

### Production Cycle — Detailed View

The Production Cycle is where Ella lives. Here's the board layout:

| Column | Contains | Owner |
|--------|----------|-------|
| **Backlog** | Tasks planned but not yet in this cycle | You |
| **Todo** | Tasks assigned to this cycle, not yet started | Ella |
| **In Progress** | Ella working or AI drafting | Ella + AI |
| **In Review** | Awaiting Ella's eyes or AI processing | Ella + AI |
| **Done** | Published or final-approved | AI |
| **Blocked** | Needs intervention | You |

### Monthly Planning Cadence

1. **End of month:** Review all 4 venture boards. What shipped? What's stuck?
2. **First of month:** Set monthly content goals (e.g., "publish 8 articles this month"). This determines Ella's batch volume: 8 articles = ~16 tasks for Ella (interview + review per article) = ~3-4 batches.
3. **Every 2 weeks:** Sprint planning. Move tasks from Backlog → Todo. Sequence batches.
4. **Weekly:** Sync with Ella. Adjust based on her energy and capacity.
5. **Daily:** 2-minute scan of "Assigned to Ella" and "Blocked or Stale" views.
