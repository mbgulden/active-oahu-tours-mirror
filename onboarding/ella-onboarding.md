# Ella's Active Oahu Onboarding Guide 🏄‍♀️

Welcome, Ella! We're so glad you're here. This guide will walk you through everything you need to know to work smoothly with the Active Oahu team. Don't worry — we've designed this to be simple, flexible, and to work *with* your brain, not against it.

---

## What Is Linear?

**Linear** is the project management tool we use to track all our content tasks. Think of it as a shared to-do list where everyone can see what needs to be done, what's in progress, and what's finished.

When you open Linear, you'll see tasks (we call them "issues") assigned to you. Each task has:
- A **title** that tells you what it's about (e.g., "Interview: Mokulua Islands Kayak Guide")
- A **description** with details about what's needed
- A **status** (To Do → In Progress → In Review → Done)
- A **comments section** where you and the AI team talk back and forth

Don't stress about learning every feature — you really only need to do three things: **find your tasks, read them, and comment on them.**

---

## How the AI Swarm Works

This is the cool part. You're not working alone — you have a team of AI assistants backing you up. Here's how it flows:

1. Michael assigns a task to you in Linear
2. You read the task description and do whatever it asks (usually recording a voice memo or reviewing something)
3. You leave a **comment** on the task with your answer, feedback, or notes
4. The AI reads your comment and takes the next action — writing the article, updating the copy, moving the task forward
5. The AI may come back with follow-up questions in the comments

**You comment → AI acts.** That's the whole loop. You're the human in the loop providing real local knowledge and judgment; the AI handles the heavy lifting of writing, formatting, and publishing.

## Your Role

You have two main jobs:

### 1. Answer Interview Questions
When a content task needs your local expertise — "What's the best time of day to kayak to the Mokes?" or "Where do you actually park at Kailua Beach?" — you'll record a short voice memo (5-10 minutes) or type your answers as a comment. The AI takes your answers and turns them into polished articles.

### 2. Review Completed Work
When the AI finishes a draft, it'll post it in the task comments for your review. Your job is to read it, check it against the task requirements, and say "looks good" or "here's what needs fixing." You're the quality gate — you catch what AI misses because you actually know Oahu.

## How to Write Comments the AI Can Act On

This is the single most important skill to master. Good comments = fast results. Vague comments = everyone confused.

### ✅ Good Comments

> "The put-in spot at Kailua Beach is near the boat ramp by the parking lot at the end of Kalaheo Ave. Paddling out takes about 30-45 minutes depending on wind. Go before 9am when the water is calmest. Bring reef-safe sunscreen — it's required by law now."

Why this works: specific, factual, actionable. The AI can pull exact details straight into the article.

> "In paragraph 3, the directions say to turn left on Kalanianaole — that should be a right turn. Also, the beach park closes at sunset, not 8pm."

Why this works: points to exactly what needs changing with the correct info.

### ❌ Bad Comments

> "Just write something about kayaking, you know the usual stuff."

Too vague. The AI doesn't know what "the usual stuff" means to a local.

> "This doesn't sound right."

Which part? What should it say instead? The AI is stuck guessing.

> "Make it more authentic."

Authentic how? Give examples or specifics.

### The Golden Rule
**Be specific.** Pretend you're texting a smart but clueless friend who's never been to Oahu. Tell them exactly what they need to know.

## The 24-Hour Rule

If a task asks you to make a big call — choosing between options, approving a major direction change, green-lighting something that'll go live — **sleep on it.** Don't feel pressured to decide in the moment, even if it seems urgent.

Write a comment like: "Let me think about this overnight and get back to you." That's 100% okay and actually encouraged. You make better decisions when you've had time to sit with them.

---

## Work in Batches

Michael will assign work in sets of about 5-10 tasks at a time — never a giant pile of 50. When you sit down to work:

1. **Open Linear** and go to your assigned tasks
2. **Pick one** — start with whatever looks easiest or most interesting
3. **Read the description** carefully
4. **Do the thing** (record a voice memo, review a draft, answer questions)
5. **Comment the result** — be specific!
6. **Move on** to the next one

Don't try to do everything in one sitting. A batch of 5 tasks should take about an hour, give or take. If you're feeling tired or foggy, stop and come back later. Quality over quantity.

## Where to Get Help

- **Stuck on a task?** Comment on it in Linear — the AI can clarify or Michael will see it
- **Technical issues?** Michael is your escalation path — message him directly
- **Not sure if something is "good enough"?** Trust your gut. If it reads well to you as a local, it's probably ready
- **Need a deadline extension?** Just say so. Life happens.
