## What Is Linear?

**Linear** is our shared workspace — a smart to-do board where tasks move from idea to published content. Think of it like a kitchen: Michael puts tasks *in*, the AI preps them, you add the secret local-knowledge ingredient, and the AI plates and serves them. You never have to cook the whole meal alone.

### The Pieces of Linear You Actually Use

| Piece | What It Is | When You Touch It |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Issue** | A single task (e.g., "Record voice memo: Mokulua Islands") | Every time you work |
| **Status** | Where the task is in the pipeline | When you start or finish |
| **Comments** | The conversation thread on a task | When you give feedback |
| **Assignee** | Who the task belongs to | Michael sets this for you |
| **Cycle** | A 2-week sprint bucket | You just see the label — Michael manages it |
| **Project** | A grouping of related tasks (e.g., "Active Oahu Content") | Rarely — mostly informational |

### What You DON'T Need to Worry About

- Creating tasks or projects (Michael does that)
- Setting due dates or priorities (the AI handles scheduling)
- Navigating complex views or filters (just look at "Assigned to Me")
- Understanding every Linear button or menu

## Your Exact Workflow (6 Steps)

Here's your full loop, every single time you sit down to work:

### Step 1: Open Linear

Open Linear on your phone or computer. The fastest way is to click the link Michael sent you — it'll take you straight to your board. Bookmark it.

### Step 2: Find Your Assigned Tasks

Look at the left sidebar and click **"Assigned to me"** (or "My Issues"). This shows ONLY tasks with your name on them — no noise, no other people's work, no confusion.

You'll see a list of cards. Each card has:
- A **title** at the top (what it's about)
- A **status tag** (To Do / In Progress / Done / Blocked)
- The **project name** or cycle label underneath

### Step 3: Read the Description

Click into a task and read the description. It'll tell you exactly what's needed. Most tasks fall into one of these categories:

- **🎙️ Voice Memo:** "Record your answers to these questions about [topic]. 5-10 minutes. The AI will extract what it needs."
- **👀 Review:** "Read this draft and check: are the directions correct? Are hours of operation accurate? Does anything feel off to a local?"
- **❓ Follow-Up:** "You mentioned [thing] in your last comment — can you expand on that?"

If the description isn't clear, comment and ask. Michael or the AI will clarify.

### Step 4: Do the Thing

| Task Type | What You Do | Time |
|-----------|------------|------|
| Voice Memo | Record yourself talking through the questions. Don't script it — just talk like you're explaining to a friend. | 5-10 min |
| Review | Read the draft. Check the specific things listed in the task description. Note anything wrong. | 5-15 min |
| Follow-Up | Answer the specific question asked. One or two paragraphs is plenty. | 2-5 min |

**Important:** You don't need to write articles, format anything, or make things "publish-ready." That's the AI's job. Your job is knowledge and judgment.

### Step 5: Comment the Result

Add a comment on the task with what you did. This is the most important step — if you don't comment, the AI doesn't know to continue. More on how to write great comments in the next section.

### Step 6: Change the Status

After commenting, update the task status:

- If you recorded a voice memo or answered questions → move to **In Review** (or just **Done** if you're sure it's complete)
- If you reviewed a draft and it's good → move to **Done**
- If you reviewed a draft and it needs changes → move to **In Progress** and say what needs fixing in your comment
- If you can't do the task for some reason → move to **Blocked** and explain why

**Pro tip:** Michael set this up so the AI watches for status changes. Moving a card to Done tells the AI to proceed with the next step automatically. You don't need to @mention anyone — the system handles it.

## How to Comment: The Make-or-Break Skill

Your comments are the steering wheel. The AI reads every comment you write and acts on it. Vague comments = AI guessing = wasted time. Specific comments = AI executing = fast results.

### ✅ GOOD Comments (With Annotated Examples)

**Example 1: Voice Memo Response**

> "Just recorded a voice memo — here's the link. Covered all 8 questions about the Mokulua paddle. A few things I emphasized: the put-in is at the boat ramp by the parking lot at the end of Kalaheo Ave (NOT the main beach entrance), paddling out takes 30-45 min depending on wind, and the best time to go is before 9am when the water is glass. Also mentioned that reef-safe sunscreen is state law now — a lot of tourists don't know that."

*Why this works:* It gives the AI context for what's in the audio. The AI can cross-reference these points while transcribing and catch details it might otherwise miss. It also flags the sunscreen law — exactly the kind of local knowledge that makes content authoritative.

**Example 2: Review With Specific Fixes**

> "Read through the Lanikai draft. Three things to fix:
> - Paragraph 2: The directions say 'turn left on Kalanianaole' — it's a RIGHT turn coming from Kailua
> - Parking section: says 'plenty of street parking' — that hasn't been true since 2023. It's almost all residential permit now. Visitors should park at Kailua Beach Park and walk over, or use the limited spots on Mokulua Drive
> - Hours: says 'open until 8pm' — the beach access closes at sunset, which varies. Just say 'closes at sunset'
>
> Otherwise reads well. The wildlife section is solid."

*Why this works:* Each fix is pinpointed to a specific location in the draft with the correct replacement info. The AI doesn't have to guess what "the parking part feels wrong" means. Bonus: you affirmed what WAS good, which helps the AI preserve those sections.

**Example 3: Follow-Up That Moves Things Forward**

> "You asked about rental shops in Kailua. The main ones are Kailua Beach Adventures (on Kalaheo Ave, closest to the boat ramp — they're the biggest operation), Two Good Kayaks (smaller, more personal, on Hamakua Drive), and Kailua Sailboards & Kayaks (they've been around forever, good for windsurfing crossovers). I've used all three. KBA is best for first-timers because they do a solid safety briefing. Two Good Kayaks is better if you want to avoid the crowd. Happy to expand on any of these."

*Why this works:* Instead of just listing names, you gave context and a recommendation — the kind of nuance an AI can't generate from Google. This turns into content that actually helps readers decide.

### ❌ BAD Comments (And Why They Stall Everything)

| Bad Comment | Why It Fails | Better Version |
|------------|-------------|----------------|
| "Just write something about kayaking, you know the usual stuff." | The AI doesn't know what "usual" means to a local. It'll generate generic content that sounds like every other travel blog. | "For the kayaking piece, focus on the wind patterns — mornings are calm, trade winds pick up by 11am. Also mention that Kailua is inside a protective reef so the water stays flat, unlike North Shore." |
| "This doesn't sound right." | Which part? What should it say? The AI has to guess and will probably guess wrong, creating more back-and-forth. | "The second paragraph about water temperature says 75°F year-round. It's actually closer to 78-80°F in summer and drops to 74-76°F in winter. Fix that number." |
| "Make it more authentic." | Authentic how? This is the most frustrating feedback for an AI to receive. | "Add a line about the Kalapawai Market post-beach ritual — locals grab a coffee or sandwich there after morning sessions. That's the real Kailua experience tourists miss." |
| "Looks fine." (on something with obvious issues) | If you're tired and just approving to clear the queue, quality suffers. Better to flag one thing than rubber-stamp bad content. | "I'm running low on energy today — can I come back to this tomorrow? Want to give it a proper read." |
| (No comment — just status change) | The AI gets no signal. It can't tell if you reviewed it, skipped it, or the status change was an accident. | Always add at least one sentence: "Reviewed — directions are accurate, parking info is current. Good to publish." |

### The Golden Rule of Comments

**Write as if you're texting a smart but clueless friend who's never been to Oahu.** They're sharp, they'll execute exactly what you say, but they have zero local context unless you give it to them. Don't assume they know things. Over-explain by one sentence.

## The 24-Hour Rule (Your Emotional Authority)

You have what we call an **open Emotional Solar Plexus** — which is a fancy way of saying your best decisions come after reflection, not in the heat of the moment. This isn't a weakness; it's a design feature when you work WITH it.

### When to Use the 24-Hour Rule

Any time a task asks you to:
- Choose between two or more significant options
- Approve a major direction change
- Sign off on something that will go live to the public
- Make a call that feels high-stakes or uncomfortable

### What to Do

Comment on the task:
> "Let me sit with this overnight. I'll have a clear answer for you tomorrow."

That's it. That comment is 100% acceptable and actually *encouraged*. You are never expected to make a snap decision. The AI and Michael would rather wait 24 hours for the right call than get a rushed answer that creates rework.

### What the 24-Hour Rule Is NOT

- It's not a way to avoid tasks indefinitely. If you need more than a day, say so: "I need until Thursday on this one."
- It's not for small, obvious things. If the task is "does this street name look right?" — just answer it.
- It's not a sign you're slow or indecisive. It's a deliberate workflow designed around how you operate best.

## Working With AI: The Loop Explained

You might be wondering: "Do I need to talk to the AI directly? Learn a special format? Use some chatbot?"

**No.** You never talk to the AI directly. You just comment on Linear tasks. The AI reads your comments and acts. Here's the full loop:

```
Michael creates task → AI generates draft or questions
        ↓
You read the task description
        ↓
You do the thing (voice memo, review, etc.)
        ↓
You COMMENT on the task with your result
        ↓
AI reads your comment → AI takes the next action
        ↓
AI posts updated version in the comments
        ↓
You review → comment → loop continues
        ↓
You mark as Done
```

### You Comment → AI Acts

That's the entire relationship. You never need to:
- Send a separate message to an AI
- Use a different tool or app
- Format anything specially
- Worry about triggering the right automation

Just comment. The AI watches. Things progress.

### If Nothing Happens After You Comment

Give it a few hours. If nothing happens by the next day:
1. Check that you changed the status (the AI may be waiting on a status signal)
2. Add another comment: "Following up — is this stuck?"
3. If still nothing after another day, ping Michael directly

## The Interview Pipeline (Your Main Workflow)

About 70% of your tasks will follow this pattern. Understanding it deeply means you'll fly through your batches.

### The Full Pipeline, Step by Step

```
PHASE 1: AI GENERATES QUESTIONS
Michael sets up a task → AI reads the topic and creates 7-8 specific interview
questions → task appears in your "Assigned to me" with status "To Do"

PHASE 2: YOU RECORD ANSWERS
You read the questions → You record a 5-10 minute voice memo → You comment
with the audio file and a brief summary → You move status to "In Review"

PHASE 3: AI DRAFTS THE PAGE
AI transcribes your audio → AI combines your local knowledge with research →
AI writes a complete draft → AI posts draft in task comments → Status moves
to "In Review" and shows up in your feed

PHASE 4: YOU REVIEW
You read the draft → You check against the task requirements → You note
corrections or approve → You comment → Status moves to "In Progress" (if
changes needed) or "Done" (if approved)

PHASE 5: FINAL PUBLISH (AI handles this)
AI applies your changes → AI does final formatting and SEO → AI marks
complete → Content goes into the publishing queue
```

### Voice Memo Recording Tips

- **Don't script it.** Just talk naturally. The AI is better at extracting from conversational speech than from stiff, rehearsed answers.
- **5-10 minutes** is the sweet spot. If you go to 15 minutes, that's fine. If you hit 20, you might be over-detailing.
- **Stories are gold.** If a question makes you think of a specific experience, tell the story. "One time I was paddling out and..." — that's the content that no other website has.
- **It's okay to skip.** If you don't have a strong answer to a question, just say "I don't have much on that one" and move on. The AI will fill gaps with research.
- **Be conversational.** "Oh, and another thing —" is fine. The AI handles structure.

### Reviewing AI Drafts: Your Checklist

When you open a draft to review, ask yourself:

- [ ] **Are the directions correct?** Left vs. right, street names, landmarks.
- [ ] **Are the hours/availability accurate?** Open times, seasonal closures, things that changed recently.
- [ ] **Does the parking/location info match reality?** The AI gets this wrong most often.
- [ ] **Does anything "feel off"?** Trust your gut. If a description of Kailua Beach doesn't sound like Kailua Beach, flag it even if you can't pinpoint why.
- [ ] **Are there any legal/rule things to mention?** Reef-safe sunscreen law, beach access rules, no-drone zones.
- [ ] **Would a local reader say "yep, they actually know what they're talking about"?** This is the ultimate test.

## Batch Size: 5 Tasks, Always 5

Michael will assign work in batches of exactly **5 tasks**. Never more. Here's why and how to work with this:

### Why 5?

- **5 tasks = ~1 hour of focused work.** You can do it in one sitting without burning out.
- **5 tasks = visible progress.** You finish a batch, you feel accomplished. 30 tasks = you feel like you're drowning.
- **5 tasks = the AI can process in parallel.** While you're on task 2, the AI is already working on your output from task 1.

### How to Work a Batch

1. **Open all 5 tasks.** Skim the titles. Get a sense of what's coming.
2. **Pick the easiest one first.** Build momentum.
3. **One at a time.** Don't context-switch. Complete the full loop (read → do → comment → change status) before opening the next task.
4. **If a task feels hard or draining, skip it.** Come back to it last or flag it for help.
5. **When you finish all 5, you're done.** Don't ask for more. Michael will assign the next batch when it's ready.
6. **If you finish a batch and want more same-day, message Michael.** Sometimes there's a second batch ready. But never feel pressure to ask.

### What If I Can't Finish a Batch?

- **Life happens.** Comment on the remaining tasks: "Got through 3 of 5 — I'll finish the other 2 tomorrow."
- **Don't ghost.** A quick status comment is infinitely better than silence.
- **Don't force it.** If you're tired, foggy, or not in a good headspace, your reviews won't be sharp. Come back fresh.

## Statuses: What Each One Means

Linear uses statuses to track where every task is. Here's exactly what they mean for YOU:

| Status | Meaning | When YOU Use It |
|--------|---------|-----------------|
| **Backlog** | Not ready yet. Still being planned. | Never — Michael sets this. If you see a task here, don't touch it. |
| **Todo** | Ready for you to work on. Fresh task. | This is where tasks land when Michael assigns them. Start here. |
| **In Progress** | You're working on it right now, or sent it back for revisions. | Move to this when you START working. Move to this when you reviewed a draft and it needs changes from the AI. |
| **In Review** | You've done your part and the AI is working on the next step. | Move to this after you submit a voice memo or answer questions. The AI picks it up from here. |
| **Done** | Complete. Nothing more needed. | Move to this when you've reviewed a draft and it's good to go. Also use this for voice memos where you're confident no follow-up is needed. |
| **Blocked** | Can't proceed. Needs input or help. | Use this if: you don't understand the task, you need something you don't have, something external is preventing progress. Always add a comment explaining WHY it's blocked. |

### Status Flow Cheat Sheet

```
Fresh task → Todo
    ↓
You start working → In Progress
    ↓
You submit voice memo/answers → In Review  (AI works here)
    ↓
AI returns draft for your review → In Progress  (you review here)
    ↓
You approve → Done
```

## When to Escalate to Michael

Most things you can handle in the task comments. But some situations need Michael directly:

### Escalate When:

- **The task description is genuinely confusing** and the AI's clarification doesn't help
- **You've been blocked for more than 2 days** with no movement
- **You discover something that changes the scope** — e.g., "this beach is closed for renovation until October, so this article doesn't make sense right now"
- **You're feeling overwhelmed** and need the batch size reduced
- **A technical issue** prevents you from accessing Linear, recording audio, or uploading files
- **You need time off** — planned or unplanned. A quick heads-up keeps the pipeline adjusted.
- **You have an idea for new content** that's outside your assigned tasks — "we should do a piece on X because Y"

### Don't Escalate When:

- You need clarification on a task → comment on the task itself
- You're running a day behind → just update the task status and keep going
- You're unsure if a review is good enough → trust your gut, submit it, the AI will ask if something's missing
- You want to change the ORDER you do tasks in your batch → that's entirely your call

### How to Escalate

DM Michael with:
- **The task ID** (e.g., GRO-142) so he can find it instantly
- **One sentence** on what the issue is
- **What you need** — clarification, unblocking, reduced workload, etc.

Example: "Hey! GRO-144 is confusing — the description says review a draft but there's no draft attached. Can you check?"

## Daily & Weekly Rhythm

### Daily (When You Have a Batch Active)

- **Morning check:** Open Linear for 2 minutes. See if any tasks moved to "In Progress" (meaning the AI returned something for your review).
- **Work session:** When you have energy and time, knock out 2-5 tasks from your current batch.
- **End of session:** Make sure every task you touched has a comment and the correct status.

### Weekly

- **Sync with Michael:** A casual 10-minute check-in. He'll tell you what's coming next, celebrate wins, and check how you're feeling.
- **Batch turnover:** Most batches are designed to be completed within a week. If you finish early, enjoy the gap. If you're running behind, communicate.

### What NOT to Do

- **Don't check Linear at night or on weekends.** This isn't an always-on job. The AI works 24/7; you don't need to.
- **Don't work on tasks that aren't in your "Assigned to me" view.** If a task doesn't have your name, it's not yours.
- **Don't worry about "falling behind."** The batch system prevents pile-ups. If you're behind, Michael will notice and adjust — you don't need to stress about it.
