## GRO-142: Mokulua Islands Kayak Guide

### 1. Describe the paddle out to the Mokes step by step.
The best place to put in at Kailua Beach Park is near the boat ramp at the southern end of the parking lot or via the Ka’elepulu Canal. If you start in the canal, the calm waters allow you to find your rhythm before hitting the ocean. Once you reach the beach, walk your kayak out until you're about waist-deep to clear the shore break, then hop in. Sight the two islands directly offshore; you're heading for Moku Nui, the larger one on the left. The paddle typically takes 45 to 60 minutes for an average paddler, depending on the trade winds.

### 2. What are the water conditions like on a typical day?
Expect consistent trade winds between 15-25 mph. The water inside the reef is relatively protected and calm, but as you approach the islands, you'll feel the ocean swell. I'd tell someone *not* to go if there's a high north or east swell, as landing on the rocky shore of Moku Nui becomes dangerous, or if the trade winds are gusting over 25 mph, making the return trip grueling.

### 3. What's the best time of day to paddle out and why?
Morning is king. Launching before 9:00 AM is ideal because the water is often "glassy" and the trade winds haven't fully kicked in yet. By 11:00 AM or Noon, the winds pick up, which can make the paddle back to Kailua a real struggle. Plus, you beat the heat and the larger midday crowds.

### 4. What should people know about landing on the island?
You are only permitted to land on the sandy beach of Moku Nui (the larger island). Moku Iki is a strictly off-limits seabird sanctuary. Once you land, secure your kayak well above the high-tide line. The terrain is a mix of soft sand and sharp volcanic rock, so keep your shoes on if you're exploring the tide pools or walking to the "Queen’s Bath" on the backside.

### 5. What wildlife are you actually likely to see?
It’s common to see Hawaiian Green Sea Turtles (Honu) popping their heads up along the way. On the island, you'll see Wedge-tailed Shearwaters nesting in the ground (watch your step!) and often find Hawaiian Monk Seals basking on the sand. Please stay at least 50 feet away from the seals—it's the law and for your safety.

### 6. What do you bring with you that people wouldn't think of?
Water shoes are a must for the rocky parts of the island. Also, bring a light long-sleeve rash guard—even if it's hot, the sun reflection off the water is intense. Lastly, bring a small trash bag; there are no bins on the island, and locals really appreciate it when visitors pick up any "drift" plastic they find.

### 7. What's the one thing tourists always get wrong about this paddle?
They underestimate the wind on the way back. It feels easy paddling out with the wind at your back, but that means you're fighting it the whole way home. Save some energy for the return leg!

### 8. Describe your most memorable trip out there.
Once, during a very calm morning paddle, a mother and calf humpback whale breached about 200 yards away from my kayak. It was outside the reef near the Mokes. The sound of the breach in the quiet morning air was something I'll never forget. It reminds you how much life is teeming just below the surface here.

## GRO-143: Lanikai Beach Guide

### 1. Walk me through accessing Lanikai Beach.
Lanikai is a residential neighborhood, so there's no parking lot. You have to find street parking on Mokulua Drive or the side streets. Be extremely careful not to block driveways or fire hydrants—enforcement is very strict. The "real" access points are narrow sandy paths between houses marked with small blue "Public Beach Access" signs. The one at the end of Mokulua Drive is the most popular.

### 2. What makes Lanikai different from Kailua Beach next door?
Kailua is a "park"—it has lifeguards, restrooms, showers, and a huge parking lot. Lanikai is a "neighborhood beach." It's narrower, has zero facilities, and feels more intimate. The sand at Lanikai is also slightly finer, like powdered sugar, and the view of the Mokes is much closer and more iconic.

### 3. What time of day is Lanikai at its best and worst?
Best: Sunrise. Seeing the sun come up between the Mokes is a spiritual experience. Early morning (before 8 AM) is also great for parking. Worst: 11:00 AM on a Saturday. It’s packed, there’s no shade, and you’ll spend 45 minutes looking for a parking spot.

### 4. What are the actual facilities (or lack thereof)?
There are NO facilities. No toilets, no showers, no water fountains. If you need a restroom, you have to walk or drive back to Kailua Beach Park. Plan your hydration and bathroom breaks accordingly!

### 5. Where's the best swimming at Lanikai?
The middle section of the beach usually has the calmest entry and the fewest rocks. The water is shallow and protected by the reef, making it like a giant swimming pool. Watch out for the ends of the beach where currents can be a bit stronger near the points.

### 6. What's the Pillbox Hike connection?
The Lanikai Pillbox (Kaiwa Ridge) trailhead is on Kaelepulu Drive. Many people hike it for the view of the beach they're about to visit. Logistically, I recommend parking at Kailua Beach Park, walking to the hike, then walking to Lanikai Beach after. It’s a lot of walking, but it beats getting a $60 parking ticket in the neighborhood.

### 7. What do you wish tourists understood about respecting this beach?
It's a residential area. Please keep your noise down, especially in the early morning. Don't leave trash on the paths, and don't use people's private garden hoses to rinse off—it happens more than you'd think!

## GRO-144: Kailua Beach Park Guide

### 1. Describe the full Kailua Beach Park layout.
It’s a massive park. At the south end, you have the boat ramp and the main parking lot. Moving north, there are three main pavilion areas with restrooms and showers. There's a huge grassy lawn in the middle for picnics and then the beach stretches for over two miles toward Kalama Beach.

### 2. What are the best spots for different activities?
Kayakers and SUPers should head to the boat ramp at the south end for the easiest launch. Families with small kids usually set up near the first lifeguard stand where the water is calmest. Windsurfers and kitesurfers typically head further north where the wind is more consistent and there are fewer swimmers.

### 3. How does Kailua change with the seasons?
Summer is calm and perfect for long distance paddling. Winter brings a bit more swell and stronger trade winds, but because it's on the Windward side, it rarely gets the massive, unrideable waves you see on the North Shore. The crowd also thins out a bit in the "off-season" months like October or February.

### 4. What's the parking situation honestly like?
On a weekday, it’s fine if you arrive by 10 AM. On a weekend or holiday, the main lot is full by 9 AM. If the main lot is full, try the Kalama Beach Park lot just up the road—it’s a short walk back.

### 5. Food and supplies near the beach.
Locals hit Kalapawai Market at the entrance to the beach for coffee and sandwiches. If you need a full lunch, Kono’s in Kailua Town is the spot for pulled pork. For forgotten sunscreen or cheap towels, there’s a Target and a Longs Drugs in town, just a 3-minute drive away.

### 6. What's the Kalapawai Market connection?
It’s the heart of the Kailua beach community. Grabbing a "Deli" sandwich and a coffee there before heading to the sand is a ritual. It’s been there since 1932 and really captures the vibe of the town.

### 7. Any safety things people should know?
Portuguese man o' war (jellyfish) wash in when the trade winds are strong—look for purple bubbles on the sand. Also, the current near the boat ramp can be tricky when the tide is changing. Always check the "conditions" board at the lifeguard towers.

### 8. What makes Kailua Beach special beyond the obvious?
It’s the community. You’ll see the same people walking their dogs or paddling their OC-1s every single morning. It’s a world-class beach that still feels like a local neighborhood spot if you're there at 7 AM.

## GRO-145: Why Kayak Kailua

### 1. What makes Kailua Bay uniquely suited for kayaking?
The geographic setup is perfect. We have a massive offshore reef that acts as a breakwater, so the inner bay stays relatively flat even when the ocean is moody. Having Moku Nui and Popoia Island as actual destinations to paddle to gives the trip a "mission" and a reward you don't get at most other beaches.

### 2. Describe the different routes you can take from Kailua.
- **The Mokes:** For the adventurous. It’s a 1-hour paddle each way to a bird sanctuary with a hidden beach.
- **Flat Island (Popoia):** The "Beginner Plus" route. Only 15-20 minutes out, easy landing, great for seeing birds and turtles.
- **Lanikai Coastal:** Stay inside the reef and paddle south along the Lanikai shoreline for the best views of the multi-million dollar homes and the turquoise water.

### 3. What skill level do you actually need?
A complete beginner can absolutely kayak Kailua, especially if they stay in the bay or go to Flat Island. For the Mokes, you need a bit of physical stamina. I'd say "maybe not" to someone who isn't a comfortable swimmer or if the winds are over 20 mph.

### 4. How does kayaking Kailua compare to other Oahu kayak experiences?
North Shore (Haleiwa) is great for river paddling, but the ocean there can be too rough in winter. Kaneohe Bay is beautiful and has the sandbar, but it's much larger and feels more like a boat harbor. Kailua offers the perfect mix of "ocean adventure" and "safety."

### 5. What's the experience like for someone who's never kayaked before?
They’re usually nervous at the boat ramp, worried about tipping. Then, as they get 100 yards out and realize how stable the tandem kayaks are, they relax. By the time they see their first sea turtle, the nerves are gone and they’re just in awe of the mountains.

### 6. What wildlife encounters make Kailua kayaking special?
Turtles are almost guaranteed if you know where to look. I've also seen manta rays gliding under the kayak in the deeper parts of the bay. It’s the sheer variety—birds, fish, turtles, and seals—all in one paddle.

### 7. What's the best weather day for kayaking Kailua?
A "Light Trades" day. Winds around 5-10 mph from the East, high clouds to keep the sun from being too punishing, and a low swell. That’s a 10/10 day.

### 8. Why do YOU personally choose to kayak Kailua?
It’s the scale of it. You can be a mile offshore, looking back at the Koolau Mountains, and feel completely removed from the world, yet you're just minutes away from a great cup of coffee in Kailua Town.

## GRO-146: Oahu Kayaking Beginner Tips

### 1. What do you wish someone had told you before your first time?
"It's a leg workout, not just arms." If you only use your arms, you'll be exhausted in 20 minutes. Use your core and rotate your torso. Also, wear more sunscreen than you think you need—the reflection off the water is a double dose.

### 2. What's the most common beginner mistake?
Paddling with the wind on the way out and not realizing they have to fight it to get back. Always check which way the wind is blowing and try to start your paddle *into* the wind so the return trip is the easy part.

### 3. What should a beginner actually wear?
A swim top or rash guard, board shorts, and a hat with a chin strap (so it doesn't blow away). Avoid cotton—it gets heavy and cold when wet. polarized sunglasses are a game changer for seeing the reef and turtles.

### 4. How do you read the water conditions as a beginner?
Look for "whitecaps"—if you see lots of white foam on the waves, it's windy. Look at the reef line; if there are big crashing waves there, the swell is high. Use an app like Windy or Surfline, or just ask the rental shop guys; they live for this.

### 5. What's the minimum gear someone needs?
A PFD (Life Vest), a paddle with a leash, and a dry bag for your keys/phone. Renting is 100% better for beginners because you get the right boat for the conditions and don't have to worry about the transport.

### 6. How do you handle capsizing?
Don't panic! The water is warm and you're wearing a life vest. In a tandem, have one person hold the boat steady while the other crawls in from the middle/side. It’s actually a great way to cool off once you realize how easy it is to get back in.

### 7. What are the reef etiquette rules?
Never, ever step on the coral—it’s a living animal and you’ll kill it (and cut your foot). Only step on clear sand. Stay at least 10 feet away from turtles and never touch them. We're guests in their home.

### 8. How do you build up from beginner to confident?
Start with a paddle in the Kaelepulu Canal or along the Kailua shoreline. Once that’s easy, try the 15-minute hop to Flat Island. After you've mastered a landing there, you're ready for the Mokuluas.

## GRO-147: Best Oahu Kayak Rentals

### 1. What do you actually look for in a kayak rental operation?
Safety orientation is #1. If they just hand you a boat and point to the ocean, go somewhere else. Look for quality gear—comfortable seats, sturdy paddles, and a shop that provides the legal permits you need for the islands.

### 2. Walk me through the Kailua-based rental options.
- **Active Oahu:** Great because they offer delivery or e-bike trailer options, and their shop is away from the main beach chaos but still close.
- **Kailua Beach Adventures:** The biggest player, right across from the beach. Very professional but can be very busy.
- **Two Good Kayaks:** A bit more of a "local" small-business feel.

### 3. What's the real difference between renting and doing a tour?
A tour gives you a lifeguard-certified guide who knows the history, the best snorkel spots, and handles all the navigation. Renting is for those who want to go at their own pace and spend 3 hours napping on the island if they want.

### 4. How far in advance do you actually need to book?
In the summer (June-August) or over Christmas, book at least 2 weeks out. For a random Tuesday in October? You can usually call the day before or even walk in.

### 5. What's included in a typical rental vs. what do they upsell you on?
Included: Boat, paddles, life vests, dry bags, and roof pads/straps. Upsells: Snorkel gear (highly recommended for the Mokes), dry bags for your phone, and high-end coolers.

### 6. Are there rental options outside of Kailua worth considering?
Yes! He'eia Kea Pier in Kaneohe is the place to rent if you want to see the Sandbar. Haleiwa on the North Shore is best for river kayaking to see turtles.

### 7. What questions should someone ask a rental company before booking?
1. Are landing permits for the Mokulua Islands included?
2. Do you provide a safety briefing and route map?
3. What is your rescue policy if we get stuck?
4. Do you provide foam pads and straps for my rental car?
5. Is there a safe place to park my car while I'm out?
