## Outline

1. **H1: Snorkel Oahu — The Complete Guide**
2. **Intro** — Oahu has world-class snorkeling for all experience levels. From calm bays to sea turtle havens, here's where to go.
3. **Top Snorkel Spots**
   - **Hanauma Bay Nature Preserve** — Most famous, protected reef, must reserve, $25 entry, closed Mon-Tue. Best for beginners.
   - **Sharks Cove (Pupukea)** — North Shore tide pools, excellent visibility summer. Free entry, rocky entry. Best for experienced snorkelers.
   - **Lanikai Beach** — Calm turquoise water, coral heads, turtles. Free, parking limited. Best for couples.
   - **Ko Olina Lagoons** — Man-made lagoons, protected, family-friendly. Free, resort area. Best for families/kids.
   - **Electric Beach (Kahe Point)** — Warm water outflow, lots of fish, turtles, sometimes dolphins. Requires swim out. Best for strong swimmers.
   - **Turtle Canyon** — Offshore snorkel spot, guaranteed sea turtles (honu). Best on boat tour.
4. **When to Go** — Summer (May-Oct) = calm waters, best visibility. Winter = bigger surf, North Shore not recommended. Morning is always best (calmest water).
5. **Gear: Rent vs Bring**
   - Mask, snorkel, fins — rent from Active Oahu (delivered to your accommodation)
   - Reef-safe sunscreen (required at Hanauma Bay, leave no trace)
   - Water shoes for rocky entries
6. **Safety Tips**
   - Never snorkel alone
   - Check current conditions (Hawaii Beach Safety website)
   - Respect marine life (don't touch turtles, coral)
   - Stay hydrated, take breaks
7. **Snorkeling with Turtles** — Honu (green sea turtles) etiquette: 10 ft distance, don't chase, don't block their path to surface
8. **What to Look For** — Honu (green sea turtles), honuʻea (hawksbill), manō (reef sharks — harmless), humuhumunukunukuāpuaʻa (state fish), manta rays at night
9. **Beyond Snorkeling** — Combine snorkeling with kayaking, paddleboarding, or e-biking for a full day
